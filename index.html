The problem

Everyone is using AI. Nobody owns it.

Teams buy tools on their own. Pilots start and stall. Nobody has said what data can go where, and the board keeps asking for the plan.

Who decides which AI projects get funded?

What are people allowed to share with AI tools?

How will you know if any of it is paying off?

A full-time Chief AI Officer is expensive and hard to find. A fractional one gives you the same leadership, for the days you need.

What I do

What a fractional Chief AI Officer does.

Six responsibilities, weighted to what your business needs now. Most engagements start with the first two.

01

AI strategy and roadmap

Pick the few AI opportunities tied to your goals, each with an owner, a budget and a target.

02

Governance and policy

Set the rules on approved tools, data and risk, so people can use AI with confidence.

03

Tools and vendors

Decide what to buy, what to build and what to ignore, and hold suppliers to what they promise.

04

Delivery oversight

Keep AI projects moving, unblock teams and measure what each one returns.

05

Team capability

Train leaders and teams to use AI in their own work, and help you hire the right people.

06

Board reporting

Give the board a clear view of progress, risk and return, in plain language.

How it works

Start small. Stay as long as it's useful.

Each step stands on its own. The retainer is sized to your business and agreed after the sprint.

  1. 01 — SPRINT

    Two-week readiness sprint

    I interview your leaders, review how AI is used today and give you a prioritised roadmap. You can stop there.

  2. 02 — RETAINER

    A monthly retainer

    A set number of days each month as your AI lead: in leadership meetings, owning the roadmap and unblocking teams.

  3. 03 — HANDOVER

    Handover

    When you're ready, I help you hire or grow a full-time AI lead and hand the work over.
